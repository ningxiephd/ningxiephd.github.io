Serving the Community

Service Grounded in Listening, Respect, and Follow Through

My approach to community facing work is grounded in cultural humility, relationship building, and respect for the knowledge people bring from their own lived experiences.

Across university, nonprofit, teaching, mentoring, and international settings, I have worked with people from varied cultural and institutional backgrounds. These experiences have reinforced that trust is built through careful listening, clarity about roles and expectations, and consistent follow through.

Community Service

Ark of Compassion

For several years, I have volunteered with Ark of Compassion, participating regularly in online peer support meetings serving Asian immigrants experiencing psychological distress and difficult life circumstances. My role has centered on listening, mutual encouragement, coping, and wellbeing.

Community Fundraising and Support

I organized a fundraising initiative for a former teacher diagnosed with leukemia, coordinating more than 40 classmates, arranging visits and a recognition gathering, and helping bring broader attention to the effort.

University and Cross Cultural Engagement

At the University of Louisville, I worked extensively with international students and families and represented the College of Business at community facing, professional, educational, and international events. I completed professional development related to implicit bias, equitable admissions practices, and serving diverse and historically underserved populations.

I have worked with students and institutional partners from many countries, particularly across Asia and Africa. That experience taught me not to treat broad demographic categories as interchangeable. Language, history, migration pathway, family structure, religion, culture, and institutional trust can all shape how people experience the same system.

Teaching, Mentoring, and Public Service

I have taught at Michigan State University and the University of Maryland and supported students through research, coursework, professional preparation, and institutional navigation. At Louisville, I onboarded and trained two student interns. At Maryland, I served as a teaching center mentor for newer international teaching assistants. Earlier, I also organized and hosted a university panel on graduate school and career pathways attended by more than 300 students.

How I Approach Community Partnership

I do not treat professional or cross cultural experience as a substitute for a community's own expertise. My goal is to enter community partnered work with humility, listen carefully, make expectations and decision processes clear, respect lived knowledge, and follow through on commitments. I am especially interested in models in which communities help shape priorities, interpretation, dissemination, and the use of research.
