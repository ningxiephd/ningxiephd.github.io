Research

Trust, Risk Perception, and Health Decisions

My research asks how communication shapes what people trust, how they understand risk, and what they decide to do in consequential health contexts.

The central through line is trust in health and risk communication. I examine how message features, source characteristics, institutions, social environments, and emerging technologies influence credibility judgments, risk perception, engagement, and health decisions.

Core Research Model

Communication & ContextMessage design, source characteristics, institutions, social environments, and technology.
Trust & Risk PerceptionTrust, distrust, credibility, perceived vulnerability, uncertainty, and interpretation of evidence.
Health DecisionsInformation seeking, engagement, prevention decisions, and willingness to act on evidence.

Current and Emerging Health Contexts

Alcohol and Cancer Risk

I am developing work on how communication can improve understanding of alcohol related cancer risk and support informed prevention decisions.

Cancer Prevention Communication

I am interested in how trust, uncertainty, risk framing, and source credibility shape the use of cancer prevention information.

Substance Risk Communication

My broader prevention agenda includes communication about alcohol and other substance related risks, social norms, and behavioral decisions.

AI and Emerging Technology

I treat AI as an extension of the trust question: how do technology mediated sources affect credibility, understanding, engagement, and decision making?

Methods

I use quantitative methods including surveys, experiments, scale development, regression based modeling, structural equation modeling, bootstrap inference, and multigroup analysis. My graduate training includes a minor/certificate in Measurement, Statistics, and Evaluation from the University of Maryland.
