Research

Trust, risk perception, and health decisions

My research examines how communication shapes trust, risk perception, and decision making in health and other high consequence contexts. The central through line is how people evaluate information, institutions, and communicators, and how those judgments influence what they believe and what they do.

Trust in health and risk communication

Trust, distrust, institutional credibility, message features, and risk perception.

Health communication and prevention

Cancer prevention communication, alcohol and substance risk communication, and community crisis resilience.

Emerging technologies

AI and other technologies as sources, tools, and communication environments that may shape trust and health decisions.

Methods

Surveys, experiments, quantitative modeling, structural equation modeling, missing data methods, bootstrap inference, and multigroup analysis.

Research direction: Communication and institutional context → trust and risk perception → health decisions.

I also contribute to multidisciplinary health research spanning public health, epidemiology, oncology, neurology, surgery, nursing, pharmacy, and biomedical sciences. That work has resulted in five peer reviewed health related publications and has strengthened my experience working across disciplinary and institutional boundaries.

Grant & Research Development

From opportunity to organized execution

I approach research development as a connected process rather than a sequence of isolated tasks. Strong programs need clear priorities, disciplined coordination, visible decisions, usable information, and a way to carry lessons forward.

1. ClarifyDefine the opportunity, purpose, decision criteria, roles, and critical requirements.
2. CoordinateAlign people, timelines, materials, dependencies, and responsibilities across teams.
3. Make VisibleUse practical dashboards, trackers, decision logs, and reporting structures to surface progress and risk.
4. Learn & ReuseCapture feedback, patterns, templates, and lessons so the next cycle starts stronger.

Program operations and coordination

At the University of Louisville College of Business, I spent nearly five years supporting graduate and professional program growth, institutional partnerships, recruitment pipelines, events, and cross unit coordination. I worked across admissions, student services, international affairs, career services, marketing, academic leadership, external organizations, and university partners while managing the detailed execution that keeps complex initiatives moving.

Proposal and funding experience

I have served as Co Principal Investigator on a $100,000 Sony Research Award proposal and as Principal Investigator on an Arthur W. Page Center foundation proposal. My work has included project rationale, scope, methods, timelines, budgets and budget justification, data collection plans, dissemination planning, and proposal editing. I also maintain an active funding intelligence practice, including monitoring NIH, NCI, NIAAA, foundation, federal, and international opportunities relevant to my work.

Portfolio visibility and learning infrastructure

My approach to complex program management emphasizes simple systems that make work visible and reusable. Examples include integrated calendars, milestone and deliverable tracking, issue and decision logs, consistent templates, shared repositories, technical assistance records, and structured after action learning. These tools support accountability while reducing the need to recreate processes from scratch.

I have not yet administered a national grantmaking portfolio. My experience is adjacent and transferable: university program operations, externally funded collaborative research, proposal development, multi stakeholder coordination, documentation, and building systems that support reliable execution and continuous learning.

Serving the Community

Relationship building grounded in respect, listening, and follow through

My approach to community facing work is grounded in cultural humility and respect for the knowledge people bring from their own lived experiences. I do not treat one population, identity, or institutional context as interchangeable with another.

Community service and peer support

For several years, I have volunteered with Ark of Compassion, participating in online peer support meetings serving Asian immigrants experiencing psychological distress and difficult life circumstances. The experience has reinforced the importance of listening, dignity, mutual support, and meeting people where they are.

Cross cultural and university engagement

At the University of Louisville, I worked extensively with international students and institutional partners from varied cultural backgrounds, represented the university at community facing and professional events, and helped students and families navigate unfamiliar academic and professional systems.

Teaching, mentoring, and service leadership

I have taught at Michigan State University and the University of Maryland, mentored students and newer international teaching assistants, trained student interns, organized a 300 plus attendee university panel on graduate education and careers, and led a fundraising initiative for a former teacher diagnosed with leukemia.

Across these settings, I have learned that trust is built through clarity, reciprocity, consistency, and follow through. Those principles shape both my community service and my professional approach to partnerships.
