## Research Areas

### Health and Risk Communication

My emerging research agenda investigates how trust influences the ways people perceive health risks, evaluate health information, and make health-related decisions. A developing area of interest is alcohol-related cancer risk communication. I am particularly interested in understanding how message characteristics and trust in information sources influence risk perceptions and preventive decisions. My long-term goal is to develop effective communication strategies that support informed health decisions across diverse populations, including historically marginalized communities.

### Crisis Communication and Organization-Public Relationships

My research in strategic communication focuses on the role of emotions in organizational communication, particularly before and after crises. My work examines positive and negative dimensions of organization-public relationships, including trust and distrust, and explores how emotions shape relationships between organizations and their publics.

### Trust in Emerging Technologies

My research also examines trust in AI-generated communication. Using survey data, I am investigating how perceived transparency and empathy relate to trust and engagement. This work lays the foundation for future research on technology-mediated health interventions and digital communication strategies.

Since 2019, I have contributed to a multidisciplinary and international health research collaboration. Working alongside researchers from public health, epidemiology, medicine, and biomedical sciences, I have contributed to five peer-reviewed health-related publications. My contributions include literature synthesis, quantitative analysis, manuscript development, and substantive editing.

## Research Methods

My research draws primarily on quantitative social science methods to test communication mechanisms and behavioral outcomes, including surveys, experiments, measurement development, and structural equation modeling. I hold a Ph.D. in Communication and completed graduate training in Measurement, Statistics, and Evaluation at the University of Maryland. I have also conducted mixed-methods and qualitative studies.
